Concrete Control Joints: Why Placement and Timing Matter

Concrete moves. Joints are planned weak planes that help manage where shrinkage cracking occurs.

What joints actually do

Contraction joints encourage shrinkage cracking to occur in planned locations. They do not guarantee crack-free concrete.

Joint layout matters

Panel proportions, reentrant corners, slab thickness, restraint, and intended use affect joint planning. Random cuts are not a substitute for a thoughtful layout.

Timing matters

Cut too early and edges can ravel; cut too late and uncontrolled cracks may already form. Weather, mix, curing, and equipment influence timing.

Isolation and construction joints

Columns, walls, and other fixed elements may need isolation. Construction joints depend on pour sequence and design.

Ask about the plan

A good estimate should identify who is responsible for joint layout, cutting, and any specified sealant.
