What a well-built project starts with

Large parking lot placements demand a plan for the work you will never see after the pour. We pay close attention to proof of subgrade condition, grading, moisture conditioning, compaction, drainage, concrete delivery, finishing sequence, and joint layout. A pretty surface is only part of a parking lot that lasts.

How we approach the work

  1. Review the intended traffic, vehicle loads, site drainage, and plans with the owner or engineer.
  2. Grade, moisture-condition, and compact the prepared subgrade and specified aggregate base.
  3. Confirm paving thickness, mix requirements, reinforcement or dowel details, and isolation locations.
  4. Plan truck access, placement lanes, crew coverage, finishing equipment, curing, and sawcut timing.
  5. Finish to the specified texture, install joints, and follow the project curing requirements.
Bring us the real details.

Plans, dimensions, photographs, site address, intended use, and target schedule help us prepare a more useful proposal. Unusual loads, structural foundations, and site drainage may require approved engineering or permits.

Frequently asked questions

Can you pour 10,000 square feet at a time?

Large placements of 10,000 square feet and more are a core focus. The daily placement plan depends on access, crew size, weather, mix supply, and finishing requirements.

Is a concrete parking lot automatically a 30-year pavement?

No contractor can promise a 30-year service life without project-specific design and use conditions. We can build to an engineered pavement design targeting long service life when the plans, loading, materials, and maintenance support it.
